Customer & Profit Optimization Analysis
📌 What is this project?

This is a beginner-to-intermediate level data analytics project where I worked on a retail dataset (Superstore data) to solve real business problems.

Instead of just creating charts, the goal of this project is to:

Understand business questions
Analyze data step-by-step
Generate insights
Give recommendations
🎯 Business Problem

A retail company wants to improve its profitability.

They are facing issues like:

Some products are not profitable
Some regions are underperforming
Not all customers contribute equally

👉 My role as a data analyst is to:

Find the problems using data
Explain them clearly
Suggest improvements
📂 Dataset Used
File: Superstore Sales Dataset (Excel)
It contains:
Column	Meaning
Order Date	When the order was placed
Sales	Revenue from the order
Profit	Profit earned
Quantity	Number of items sold
Customer Name	Name of customer
Region	Location of sale
Category/Sub-Category	Product type
🧹 Step 1: Data Cleaning (Very Important)
🔹 What I did:
Loaded Excel data into Power BI
Checked for missing values
Verified data types:
Date → Order Date
Number → Sales, Profit, Quantity
Removed duplicates (if any)
🔹 Why this matters:

If data is incorrect, your analysis will also be wrong.

📊 Step 2: Creating Basic KPIs

KPIs (Key Performance Indicators) help us understand overall business performance.

🔹 Metrics Created:
Total Sales → Total revenue
Total Profit → Total earnings
Total Orders → Number of orders
Profit Margin → Profit divided by Sales
🔹 Why this matters:

These numbers give a quick overview of business health.

📉 Step 3: Profitability Analysis
🔹 What I did:
Created a bar chart of Profit by Sub-Category
Sorted it to find:
Loss-making products
High-profit products
🔹 What I learned:
Not all products are profitable
Some products consistently generate losses
🌍 Step 4: Regional Analysis
🔹 What I did:
Compared Sales and Profit across regions
🔹 What I looked for:
Regions with high sales but low profit
🔹 Why this matters:

High sales does not always mean good business performance.

📦 Step 5: Quantity vs Profit Analysis

(Note: Discount column was not available, so I used Quantity instead)

🔹 What I did:
Created a scatter plot:
X-axis → Quantity
Y-axis → Profit
🔹 What I analyzed:
Does selling more items increase profit?
🔹 Key learning:
High quantity sales do not always mean high profit
➕ Extra Metric: Profit per Unit
🔹 What I did:

Calculated:

Profit per Unit = Profit / Quantity

🔹 Why this matters:

This shows how much profit we earn per item sold.

👥 Step 6: Customer Analysis
🔹 What I did:
Created a table with:
Customer Name
Total Sales
Total Profit
🔹 What I found:
Some customers contribute much more than others
🔹 Why this matters:

Businesses should focus on valuable customers.

🧠 Step 7: Customer Segmentation
🔹 What I did:

Divided customers into:

High Value
Medium Value
Low Value

Based on their total sales.

🔹 Why this matters:

Helps in targeted marketing and retention strategies.

📊 Step 8: Dashboard Creation
🔹 Dashboard includes:
KPI Cards (Sales, Profit, Orders, Margin)
Profit by Sub-Category
Region Analysis
Quantity vs Profit
Customer Table
🔹 Goal:

Make data easy to understand for business users.

🔍 Key Insights (What I Found)
Some product categories are consistently loss-making
Selling more quantity does not always increase profit
Some regions have strong sales but weak profitability
A small number of customers generate most of the profit
💡 Business Recommendations

Based on analysis:

Reduce focus on loss-making products
Improve pricing or cost strategy in low-profit regions
Focus on high-value customers
Promote products with higher profit per unit
